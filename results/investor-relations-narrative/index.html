Results · Investor relations

From old news to the latest filing.

A listed company in India. Investors asking AI engines about it were getting old figures and a controversy that had already been resolved. Now the answer starts with the latest filing.

10 Nov 20266 min readBy the Optimizer360 team

The short answer

In this investor relations programme, the AI answer to investor questions about a listed company in India moved from old figures and a resolved controversy to the latest filing. Answer accuracy and current sources cited went up, the old controversy dropped as the headline, and investor sentiment improved. The work used public disclosures only, followed SEBI rules and had company sign-off.

What was the problem?

When investors asked AI engines about the company, the answers led with figures from older periods and with a controversy that had been resolved. The latest results were in filings and presentations, but engines were drawing on older, weaker sources instead.

The job was accuracy, not spin: make the current, public record the easiest thing for engines to find and quote.

What did we do?

All of it from public disclosures, with company sign-off before anything was published.

  • Investor site

    Results, presentations and call transcripts published as readable text, not only PDFs. A key-facts page with schema, and an FAQ answering what investors ask AI.

  • Reference sources

    Current financials and leadership proposed on the Wikipedia talk page, sourced to filings. Wikidata entity, listing and leaders kept current. Exchange and financial-portal profiles corrected.

  • Media

    Financial-media coverage after each result, from public disclosures only, plus analyst-facing explainers.

  • Forums and monitoring

    Investor forums and Reddit monitored. Factual errors answered with links to filings, never promotion. Drift alerts after every results date and corporate action.

  • Compliance

    Public disclosures only, SEBI rules followed, and company sign-off before anything was published.

How did the programme run?

  1. 01

    Scan

    Ran the questions investors ask on 8 AI engines and stored every answer, figure and source with its date.

  2. 02

    Diagnose

    Found where answers quoted old figures, which sources engines relied on, and how often the resolved controversy led.

  3. 03

    Fix

    Made the investor site readable, corrected reference sources and supported current media coverage, all signed off by the company.

  4. 04

    Prove

    Re-measured after each change and after every results date, against the Week-1 baseline.

Accuracy, not spin: we use only public disclosures, follow SEBI rules and get company sign-off before anything is published. We do not promise what engines will say, and we do not name the company.

Questions

Can you change what ChatGPT tells investors about a listed company?

We cannot control any engine. We can make the company's current, public record easy for engines to read and cite, correct reference sources with filings, and measure what the answers say every week.

Is GEO for investor relations SEBI compliant?

Our IR work uses public disclosures only, follows SEBI rules and has company sign-off before anything is published. We never publish unpublished or price-sensitive information.

Is the Meridian Industries example real?

No. Meridian Industries is a fictional company used to illustrate the pattern. The client is a listed company in India, and we do not name it.

Why do AI engines quote old figures about a company?

Engines draw on what they can find and read. If results sit only in PDFs or on hard-to-read pages, older secondary sources often fill the gap.
