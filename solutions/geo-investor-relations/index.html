Solutions · GEO for investor relations

Accuracy, not spin.

Investors now ask AI engines about your company before they open your filings. We make sure the answer reflects your latest disclosures, not last year's.

Get your AI audit →Questions, answered
The short answer

Investor relations GEO keeps what AI engines tell investors about a listed company accurate and current. It uses only public disclosures, follows SEBI rules and needs company sign-off before anything is published. The work strengthens the sources AI reads, such as filings, annual reports and call transcripts, and sets drift alerts so each new result replaces the old figures.

What does AI say about listed companies?

Ask an AI engine about a listed company and it will give a confident summary: revenue, margins, debt, management and recent news. That summary is stitched together from sources of very different ages. If the most-read sources are stale, the answer is stale, and it is delivered with the same confidence as a fresh one.

For an investor relations team this is a new kind of exposure. The answer is not on your website, you did not write it, and you may not know it exists. Investor relations GEO makes it visible, measures it every week and fixes the sources behind it.

What does AI read about your company?

These are the sources we audit and strengthen, so the latest facts are easy to find and quote.

  • Exchange filings

    Results, shareholding patterns and disclosures filed with the exchanges.

  • Annual reports

    Long, rich documents that engines often read selectively, so the key figures must be easy to extract.

  • Earnings call transcripts

    Management commentary that engines quote directly, sometimes long after it is out of date.

  • Financial media

    Business news coverage, which often outranks your own pages as a cited source.

  • Investor portals

    Data and screener sites that restate your numbers, not always the latest ones.

  • Wikipedia and forums

    Your Wikipedia article and investor forum threads, which can keep an old story alive.

What goes wrong in AI answers about listed companies?

  • Old results quoted as current

    The engine reports figures from an earlier year or quarter as if they were the latest.

  • Wrong figures

    Numbers mixed up between segments, periods or units, or copied from a portal that got them wrong.

  • A resolved controversy as the headline

    An issue closed long ago still leads the answer, without its outcome or what has happened since.

How does investor relations GEO work?

  1. 01

    Scan

    We run the questions investors actually ask, across eight AI engines, and capture each answer with its date and sources.

  2. 02

    Diagnose

    We mark every claim as current, outdated or wrong, and trace it to the source the engine relied on.

  3. 03

    Draft

    We prepare fixes from public disclosures only: clear results pages, investor FAQs, schema, transcript pages and sourced Wikipedia talk-page requests.

  4. 04

    Sign off

    Your company secretarial, legal and IR teams approve every item before anything is published.

  5. 05

    Prove

    We re-measure weekly and report accuracy, sources cited and sentiment against the Week-1 baseline.

What happens after each results announcement?

Results days are when AI answers drift most. New figures are filed, but engines keep quoting the old ones for a while. After each announcement we re-scan the investor questions and raise a drift alert wherever an answer still cites the previous period, a superseded figure or an outdated source.

Each alert comes with the source causing it and a proposed fix, ready for your sign-off. The same alerts run after other material events, such as a change in leadership or a large order you have disclosed.

Inside the lines: we use only information you have already disclosed publicly. We do not add forecasts, selective figures or promotional language, and we do not try to bury news that is accurate. The aim is an answer that matches your filings. Every item follows SEBI rules and needs company sign-off before it is published.

What changed in one investor relations programme?

For one listed company in India, the AI answer to common investor questions moved from old figures and a resolved controversy to the latest filing. Answer accuracy went up, current sources were cited more often, the old controversy stopped leading the answer, and investor sentiment in the answers improved.

Questions

Is GEO for investor relations allowed under SEBI rules?

Yes, when it is done properly. We work only from information you have already disclosed publicly, add no forecasts or promotional claims, and publish nothing without your company's sign-off. The aim is accuracy, which is what disclosure rules are there to protect.

Why does ChatGPT still quote our old financial results?

AI engines answer from the sources they find and trust, and those are often older articles, portals or transcripts. If your latest figures are hard to extract or rarely cited, the old ones win. We make the current disclosures easy to find and quote, then measure whether the answers change.

Can you remove negative news about our company from AI answers?

No. We do not bury accurate news. If a matter has been resolved, we make sure the resolution and the current facts are documented in public sources, so an engine has what it needs to give a complete and current answer.

Who approves what you publish?

Your company does. Every page, FAQ, Wikipedia talk-page request and media note goes through your IR, legal and company secretarial teams before it is published.

How quickly do AI answers update after we announce results?

It varies by engine and source, and no one can force an update. We re-scan after each announcement, flag answers still quoting the previous period and fix the sources behind them. You see the gap close, or not, in the weekly report.
