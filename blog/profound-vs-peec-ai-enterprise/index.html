Profound vs Peec AI: A Complete Comparison for Enterprise Teams (2026)

Compare Profound vs Peec AI for enterprise: 2026 pricing, AI engines tracked, SOC 2 and SSO security, agents and reviews, plus which tool fits your team.

24 Nov 202612 min readBy the Optimizer360 team

The short answer

Profound vs Peec AI comes down to scale. Profound fits large enterprises that need SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA (US healthcare data) compliance, single sign-on (SSO), crawler analytics and AI agents that act on the data. Peec AI fits teams that want published prices, unlimited seats and a fast start; its Enterprise plan closes much of the security gap.

What is the difference between Profound and Peec AI?

Profound is an enterprise AI marketing platform that tracks how answer engines describe a brand and then uses agents to create and publish content that changes those answers. Peec AI is an AI search analytics platform built for marketing teams that want clean, self-serve visibility reporting with unlimited users.

The two companies are at very different stages. Profound, founded in New York in August 2024, raised a $180M Series D at a $1.8B valuation in September 2026, co-led by Sequoia Capital and Kleiner Perkins, and says it serves over 1,000 enterprise brands. Peec AI, based in Berlin, raised a $21M Series A in November 2025, bringing total funding to $29M, and its homepage now claims more than 4,000 brands and agencies.

Why it matters: McKinsey reports that half of consumers it surveyed now intentionally seek out AI-powered search, and projects $750 billion in US revenue will flow through it by 2028. Both tools exist to measure your share of that.

How much do Profound and Peec AI cost in 2026?

Profound publishes no price for brands: its only current plan is a custom Enterprise package sold after a demo. Peec AI publishes monthly prices of $95 (Starter), $245 (Pro) and $495 (Advanced), with a custom annual Enterprise plan.

Profound. Profound's pricing FAQ states plainly that "Profound retired its Starter and Growth plans for brands in September 2026." The $399 and $499 prices tied to those plans are no longer available to new customers. Today, the Profound pricing page only lets you book a demo: there is no free-trial plan, and no agency price is listed. Agents run on credits, and higher credit allowances require a larger Enterprise package. Prices as of November 2026; check Profound's pricing page for current rates.

Peec AI. The Peec AI pricing page lists:

What an enterprise really pays. A self-serve price is rarely the enterprise price. By our own arithmetic, Peec AI Advanced with three extra models comes to $915 a month (the $495 plan plus three add-ons at $140 each) for six models, 350 prompts and three countries, and it still lacks SSO. Most enterprise teams will therefore land on Peec AI's custom Enterprise plan, which puts both vendors on a quote.

Which AI engines do Profound and Peec AI track?

Profound's Enterprise plan tracks up to 10 answer engines. Peec AI lets self-serve customers choose any 3 of 8 AI platforms, and its Enterprise plan tracks up to 26 models.

Profound's pricing page names ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, Gemini, DeepSeek, Anthropic Claude, Google AI Overviews, Exa Search, Muse Spark and Qwen. Confirm the exact list in your quote.

Peec AI's self-serve choices are ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overview, AI Mode, Gemini, Naver AI Briefing, ERNIE (Baidu) and Bing AI Mode. Note: Claude is not on that self-serve list, so teams that need Claude coverage should confirm it is in their Enterprise scope.

Engines vs models: why 26 is not 26 platforms. The two vendors count different things. Profound's 10 are separate answer engines, the AI search products people use. Peec AI's 26 Enterprise models are its 8 consumer platforms plus 18 API models, and many of those share a provider: five are OpenAI models (including ChatGPT), four are Claude models from Anthropic, and two are Mistral models. Peec AI Enterprise covers Claude only through these API models. When comparing quotes, count distinct platforms, not model entries.

For global brands, Peec AI's inclusion of Naver and ERNIE is useful for Korea and China. Profound's AI instructions page claims support for 30+ languages and 150+ regions.

Which is more enterprise-ready on security and governance?

Profound has the more complete published security posture: SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, and SSO through Security Assertion Markup Language (SAML) or OpenID Connect (OIDC). Peec AI offers SSO and a SOC 2 Type II report on its Enterprise plan, but publishes less detail.

Profound. Its enterprise page states that Profound is SOC 2 Type II compliant and supports SSO "using SAML or OIDC". Its Trust Center lets buyers request security documentation, and Profound announced HIPAA compliance for healthcare answer engine optimisation (AEO). Enterprise contracts can include a dedicated Slack channel and a specialist on a 24-hour service-level agreement (SLA).

Peec AI. Peec AI's pricing FAQ says it "holds a SOC 2 Type II report from an independent auditor", available to Custom-plan customers through sales during a vendor review. Peec AI publishes fewer security details than Profound, so ask for the report and its audit period before you sign.

Questions for your security review (both vendors):

  1. Send the SOC 2 Type II report, its audit window and any noted exceptions.
  2. Which SSO protocols are supported, and is SCIM (System for Cross-domain Identity Management) provisioning available?
  3. Where is customer data hosted, and is EU data residency available?
  4. Can roles be restricted per project, brand or market?
  5. Is there an exportable audit log of user actions?

How do Profound and Peec AI differ on data and analytics?

Profound sells data depth: browser-captured answers, prompt-volume estimates and crawler analytics from content delivery network (CDN) logs. Peec AI sells clean daily tracking with flexible exports into the business intelligence (BI) tools a team already uses.

How answers are collected. Profound says it captures AI responses "directly from the consumer browsing experience rather than from model APIs", so the data reflects what users see. Peec AI runs each prompt once every 24 hours on every selected model; its homepage doesn't say whether collection uses the interface or the API. Ask both vendors to show raw answers for the same prompt on the same day.

Prompt demand data. Profound's Prompt Volumes product is built on what it calls 2B+ real user prompts and, on Enterprise, includes custom searches, all-time history, CSV/JSON exports and API access. Ask Peec AI to show how it estimates prompt demand, so you can compare the two approaches in a demo.

Crawler analytics. Profound's Agent Analytics can connect to Akamai, AWS, Cloudflare, Fastly, Google Analytics, Google Cloud Platform, Netlify, Vercel and WordPress, according to its pricing page. Its enterprise page claims 30B+ crawler visits analysed daily. Peec AI also offers agent analytics showing how AI agents crawl your pages.

Reporting and integrations. Peec AI includes CSV export, a REST application programming interface (API) on Pro and above, a Model Context Protocol (MCP) server for assistants such as Claude, Cursor and n8n, and a Looker Studio connector (Advanced and above). For enterprises with a central BI team, that makes Peec AI data easy to pipe into existing dashboards.

Can either tool act on the data, or only report it?

Profound can act: its AI Marketer and Agents draft, refresh and publish content, metered by credits. Peec AI mainly recommends: its Actions and Agent features prioritise fixes and investigate the data, but publishing stays with your team.

Profound's Series D announcement describes AI Marketer as an agent that surfaces opportunities and deploys sub-agents, fed by a Context Manager built from your knowledge base and meeting transcripts. Before an agent runs, Profound shows an estimated credit cost, and admins can choose overage billing or a usage pause.

Peec AI's homepage positions Actions as "know what to improve, and why" and Agent as a way to investigate the data and act on it. In practice, Profound's agents can carry out a fix themselves, while Peec AI hands your team the recommendation to act on.

Neither tool decides what AI engines cite. Being named and being cited as a source are different outcomes, so it is worth understanding the difference between mentions and citations before you judge either platform's recommendations.

What do enterprise reviewers say about each tool?

Profound has a large enterprise review base on G2; Peec AI has very few enterprise reviews so far. This likely reflects each company's target market rather than product quality, since Peec AI's ratings are slightly higher overall.

Profound's G2 profile shows 4.5/5 from 1,133 reviews, with 305 reviewers at companies of more than 1,000 employees and 532 in mid-market. Peec AI's G2 profile shows 4.7/5 from 21 reviews: 14 small business, 6 mid-market and 1 enterprise.

For an enterprise buyer, the useful takeaway is reference depth. Ask Profound for enterprise references in your industry; with Peec AI, ask for references at your scale.

Profound vs Peec AI: which should an enterprise team choose?

For most enterprise teams with a security review, several markets and limited in-house capacity to act on data, Profound is the safer choice. Peec AI is the better choice when the priority is broad access across teams, predictable published pricing and a low-risk pilot.

Choose Profound if:

  • Your procurement requires SOC 2 Type II plus HIPAA, or OIDC SSO.
  • You want crawler-log analytics across a large CDN footprint.
  • You want agents to draft and publish content, and can budget for credits.
  • You need enterprise references and a written support SLA.

Choose Peec AI if:

  • You want to start this quarter on a published price, then upgrade.
  • Many teams, regions or agencies need access, and per-seat costs matter.
  • Your BI team wants data in Looker Studio, an API or via MCP.
  • You need Naver or Baidu ERNIE coverage for Asian markets.

A sensible enterprise path: pilot Peec AI Advanced for a quarter to set a baseline, then run a formal Profound evaluation if you need agents, HIPAA or deeper crawler data. If budget allows, run both in parallel on the same prompts (below).

How to run a fair two-week bake-off before you sign

Run both tools on the same prompts, engines, countries and dates, then compare accuracy rather than dashboards. Two weeks of daily data gives you enough runs to compare day-to-day variation.

  1. Pick 100–200 prompts that map to revenue: category, comparison, pricing and problem questions, in each priority language.
  2. Lock the scope: the same engines, countries and competitor set in both tools.
  3. Spot-check raw answers: each day, run five prompts by hand and compare with what each tool recorded.
  4. Score citation accuracy: does each tool identify the same cited URLs for the same answer?
  5. Test the workflow: ask each vendor to turn one visibility gap into a recommended fix, and time how long it takes.
  6. Price the full scope: request written quotes for your real prompt count, engines, countries and agent credits.

Questions

Is Profound better than Peec AI for enterprise?

For most large enterprises, yes. Profound publishes a fuller security posture (SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, SAML or OIDC SSO), tracks up to 10 engines on Enterprise, adds crawler analytics and content agents, and has 305 enterprise reviewers on G2. Peec AI is a strong option for enterprises that value published pricing, unlimited seats and BI exports, especially for a first-phase pilot.

How much does Profound cost in 2026?

Profound doesn't publish a price for brands. Since retiring its Starter and Growth plans in September 2026, its pricing page lists only a custom Enterprise package, quoted after a demo, with agent usage billed in credits. The earlier $399 and $499 plans are no longer sold to new brand customers, and its pricing page lists no free-trial plan.

How much does Peec AI cost?

As of November 2026, Peec AI lists Starter at $95 a month (50 prompts), Pro at $245 (150 prompts) and Advanced at $495 (350 prompts), each with three models of your choice and unlimited users. Extra models cost $30 to $140 a month depending on plan, annual billing saves 15%, and Enterprise is custom-priced on annual terms.

Does Peec AI have SOC 2 and SSO?

Yes, on its Enterprise plan. Peec AI's pricing page lists SSO and states that Peec AI holds a SOC 2 Type II report from an independent auditor, which Enterprise customers can obtain through sales during a vendor review. Ask for the report and its audit period directly as part of your security review.

Which tracks more AI engines, Profound or Peec AI?

Peec AI lists more, but the numbers count different things. Profound Enterprise tracks up to 10 answer engines, each a separate AI search product. Peec AI Enterprise tracks up to 26 models: 8 consumer platforms plus 18 API models, several from the same providers, such as OpenAI and Anthropic. Self-serve Peec AI plans cover 3 of the 8 platforms, and Claude isn't one of them.

Can I use Profound and Peec AI together?

You can, for example during an evaluation. One option is to run Peec AI for broad, low-cost team access while testing Profound's agents and crawler data on a smaller scope. Long term, paying for two tracking platforms is hard to justify, so use the overlap period to decide which data you trust more.
