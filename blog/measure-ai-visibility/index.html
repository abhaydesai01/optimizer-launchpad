How to measure AI visibility and share of voice

There is no ranking report for AI answers. Here is how to build a measurement system for them that a board will accept.

10 Nov 20266 min readBy the Optimizer360 team

The short answer

To measure AI visibility, build a fixed set of buyer questions (a prompt universe), run it across the AI engines your buyers use and record every answer with its date. Track four numbers against a Week-1 baseline: visibility score, citation rate, share of voice and brand sentiment. Re-run on a schedule to catch drift.

Why is AI visibility hard to measure?

Three reasons. First, AI engines publish no search volume, so nobody outside the engine knows how often a question is asked. Second, answers vary by engine, by exact wording and by day. Third, most AI engines offer brands no report showing how often they were named or cited.

So you build your own measurement. Done well, it gives a stable, repeatable view of where you stand. Done badly, it produces screenshots and anecdotes. The difference is method.

What is a prompt universe?

A prompt universe is the fixed set of questions you track. It should reflect what your buyers really ask, phrased the way they ask an AI engine. Build it from sources you can see:

  • Your SEO data: search queries, especially the longer, question-style ones.
  • Your own enquiries: what prospects ask sales, support and your WhatsApp line.
  • A category map: definitions, comparisons, shortlists, prices, locations and problems.
  • A live baseline: what the engines answer today, and which brands they name.

Rank each question by demand and winnability. Demand comes from the signals above. Winnability is how close you are to being named, and how strong the brands and sources in the current answer are. Then band the questions: Own (high demand, and you can win), Build (worth it, but needs work) and Monitor (watch, do not invest yet).

One rule: do not invent prompt volume. AI engines do not publish it. Any figure claiming a precise monthly volume for a ChatGPT prompt is an estimate, and you should know how it was made before you trust it.

What should you measure?

Four numbers, plus two ideas that hold them together. Each number answers a different question, and none is enough alone.

Visibility score
The share of tracked answers, across your prompt universe and the engines you track, in which your brand is named. Named in 40 of 100 answers means a visibility score of 40%.
Citation rate
The share of answers that cite your own pages or profiles as a source. Being named is good. Being the source the engine points to is stronger.
Share of voice
Your mentions as a share of all brand mentions in the answers for your prompt universe. It shows your position against the competitors who are named, not in isolation.
Brand sentiment
Whether answers describe you positively, neutrally or negatively, and whether the facts are right. A mention with an outdated price or an old controversy is not a win.
Week-1 baseline
The first full, dated measurement of your prompt universe, taken before any fixes. Every later report is compared with it.
Drift
A change in what engines say about you that you did not cause, such as an old price after a change, or last year's results after new ones are filed.

How do you set a Week-1 baseline?

Run the full prompt universe across every engine you track before changing anything. For each answer, record the date, the engine, the brands named and their order, the sources cited, the facts stated about you and the tone. Run each question more than once, because answers vary, and report the pattern rather than one lucky result.

Keep the raw answers. Months later, when someone asks whether the work changed anything, you can put the actual text from Week 1 next to the text from today. That persuades a board far more than a chart on its own.

How often should you re-measure?

  1. 01

    Every week

    Re-run the core questions and report against the baseline. Weekly is frequent enough to tie movement to specific fixes.

  2. 02

    After every fix you ship

    Log each change with its date: a new page, a schema update, a corrected listing, an answered review. Then check the questions it was meant to move.

  3. 03

    After business events

    Launches, price changes, results announcements and leadership changes are when drift appears. Check within days.

  4. 04

    Every quarter

    Review the prompt universe itself. Add new questions, retire stale ones and re-rank by demand and winnability.

What does good progress look like?

An example from our own work. In one programme for a residential developer in India, the visibility score rose from 32% to 58% across eight AI engines in under 2.5 months from the Week-1 baseline. Citation rate, share of voice and brand sentiment all went up, and the developer now shows up for 80 to 90 tracked buyer questions. See the full case study.

That is one programme in one category, not a benchmark. The useful lesson is the method: a fixed question set, a dated baseline, every fix logged and the same questions re-run each week, so movement can be traced to work done.

Keep a second scoreboard as well: SEO and your own enquiries. If AI visibility rises and qualified enquiries do not, something after the answer needs attention. Our leads and pipeline work traces each deal back to where demand began.

Can you track your brand in ChatGPT by hand?

For a first look, yes. Pick 20 questions, ask them in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google, and paste the answers into a spreadsheet with the date. You will learn a lot in an afternoon.

It stops scaling quickly. Eight engines, a few hundred questions, several runs each and weekly repeats add up to thousands of answers. Your own account history and location can also colour what you see. At that point you need a tool that runs clean, repeatable queries and stores every answer and source. Our platform tracks eight engines with up to 10,000 prompts per brand a day and records every citation.

Which mistakes make AI visibility data misleading?

  • Tracking only brand-name questions. Asking "What is [your brand]?" tests accuracy, not visibility. Most buying questions do not mention you, and those are where shortlists form.
  • Changing the question set every month. If the prompts move, the trend means nothing. Revise the universe quarterly and report old and new sets separately for a while.
  • One run per question. A single answer can be an outlier. Repeat runs and report the pattern.
  • Counting mentions without reading them. A mention in a list of brands to avoid is not progress. Read the sentiment and the facts, not just the count.
  • No dates on fixes. Without a dated log, you cannot tell which change moved which answer.

Measurement checklist:

  • A written prompt universe, banded Own, Build and Monitor.
  • No invented prompt volumes.
  • A dated Week-1 baseline, with the raw answers kept.
  • Four numbers: visibility score, citation rate, share of voice, sentiment.
  • A fixes log with dates.
  • Drift checks after launches, price changes and results.
  • A second scoreboard for SEO and owned enquiries.

Questions

What is AI share of voice?

AI share of voice is your brand's share of all brand mentions in AI answers for a defined set of questions. If your brand is named 100 times and competitors 300 times across the set, your share of voice is 25%.

How do I track my brand in ChatGPT?

Write down the questions your buyers ask, run them in ChatGPT on a regular schedule and record whether you are named, how you are described and which sources are cited. For many questions across several engines, use a tool that stores every answer with its date.

What is a good AI visibility score?

It depends on your category and the questions you track, so compare against your own baseline and your competitors rather than a universal number. In one real-estate programme, the score moved from 32% to 58% across eight engines in under 2.5 months.

Can I get search volume for ChatGPT prompts?

No. AI engines do not publish prompt volume. Use demand signals you can see, such as your search data and sales enquiries, and treat any precise prompt volume figure as an estimate.

Why do AI answers change from day to day?

Engines update their models and retrieval, and answers vary with wording and context. That is why each question is run more than once and tracked as a trend against a baseline, rather than judged on a single answer.
