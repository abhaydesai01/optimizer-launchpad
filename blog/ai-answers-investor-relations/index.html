AI answers and investor relations: a guide

An investor can now ask an AI engine about a listed company and get a confident summary in seconds. That summary is not always current. Here is how IR teams can keep it accurate.

10 Nov 20266 min readBy the Optimizer360 team

The short answer

AI answers about listed companies are assembled from whatever the engine finds: filings, news, old articles, forums and data sites. They can quote outdated figures or lead with a resolved controversy. Investor relations teams can improve accuracy by publishing current, clearly structured disclosures on the IR website and keeping public facts consistent, within SEBI rules.

What does ChatGPT say about your listed company?

Try it now. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity: "Is [your company] a good investment?", "What were [your company]'s latest results?" and "What are the risks of investing in [your company]?" Read the answers as an investor would.

Problems we often see in AI answers about listed companies:

  • Figures from an older year presented as current.
  • A controversy resolved long ago presented as the headline.
  • Management changes missed.
  • Segments, subsidiaries or names confused with a similarly named company.
  • Aggregator pages and forum posts cited instead of the company's own filings.

ChatGPT alone has 800 million weekly users (OpenAI, October 2025). Some of your shareholders, and some of the people deciding whether to become one, are reading these answers.

Why do AI answers about listed companies go stale?

Engines favour sources that are easy to read, widely referenced and consistent with each other. A company's latest results may sit in a PDF on the stock exchange website and an investor presentation on the IR page. An old news story about a dispute may sit on a high-authority media site, linked from dozens of other pages. The engine often finds the old story first.

IR websites can add to the problem. Many are archives of PDFs with little plain text, no dates in headings and no structured data. An engine looking for the latest revenue figure has to dig through documents, while a data aggregator with a clean table, even an outdated one, is easier to quote.

What does a stale answer look like, and how is it fixed?

Illustration only. Meridian Industries is a fictional listed company used to show the pattern. The answers described are not real engine output.

Before. Asked "What is the outlook for Meridian Industries?", an engine leads with a regulatory dispute that was settled two years ago, quotes revenue from an annual report that is two years old, and cites a forum thread and an aggregator page.

What the IR team did. It published a plain-text summary of each quarterly result on the IR site, dated and linked to the exchange filing. It added a company facts page with current leadership, segments and the status of past matters, each referencing a public disclosure. It added structured data for the organisation and its key pages. It requested factual corrections on reference sites through their proper channels, with sources. Every item had company sign-off before going live.

After. The same question returns an answer that opens with the latest filed results, describes the dispute as resolved and cites the company's own results page and exchange filing.

The same pattern played out in one of our programmes for a listed company in India. The AI answer to investor questions moved from old figures and a resolved controversy to the latest filing. Answer accuracy and the citing of current sources went up, the old controversy as headline went down, and investor sentiment improved. Read the case study.

How do you make an IR website readable by AI?

  • Plain text alongside PDFs. Publish a short HTML summary of every result, presentation and material announcement, with the key figures stated in sentences.
  • Dates everywhere. Put the period and filing date in the heading. "Q2 FY27 results, quarter ended 30 September 2026" beats "Financial results".
  • A company facts page. Leadership, board, segments, subsidiaries, registered office, listing details and auditors, kept current.
  • Answer-first FAQs. The questions investors actually ask, such as dividend history, capital allocation policy and how to contact IR, answered from public disclosures only.
  • Structured data. Organisation schema with official identifiers and links to official profiles, plus FAQ and article schema where relevant.
  • A link to the source filing. Every summary points to the original exchange filing, so engines and investors can verify it.
  • Stale pages clearly dated. Old presentations are labelled as historical, not left looking current.

Which SEBI and compliance lines must you respect?

Accuracy, not spin. For a listed company in India, anything published about financial performance or material events must be consistent with what has been disclosed to the stock exchanges under SEBI rules. GEO for investor relations creates no new information. It makes existing public disclosures easier to find, read and quote.

  • Public disclosures only. No figures, guidance or detail that has not already been disclosed.
  • No selective disclosure. Nothing appears on an IR page, a reference site or a community thread that investors could not already find in filings.
  • Company sign-off. The company secretary, compliance and IR approve every item before it goes live.
  • No promotion. No claims about share price, returns or investment merit.
  • Corrections through proper channels. Reference sites such as Wikipedia are corrected under their own rules, through talk-page requests with sources and a disclosed conflict of interest.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Your compliance team and advisers decide what applies to your company.

Which investor questions should you track?

Start with the questions investors and analysts already put to your IR team, then add the ones they are likely to ask an AI engine first. A useful starting set covers:

  • Performance: latest results, growth by segment and margins over time.
  • Capital: dividend history, debt, capital allocation and recent fundraises.
  • People: chair, managing director, CFO and board changes.
  • Risks: regulatory matters, litigation, customer concentration, and past controversies with their current status.
  • Peers: how the company compares with named listed competitors.

Phrase them the way a retail investor would type them, not the way a filing is written.

How do IR teams monitor AI answers?

Treat AI answers like any other channel investors use. Build a fixed set of investor questions covering results, dividends, management, risks, peers and past matters. Run them across the engines on a schedule, keep every answer with its date and watch for drift, especially in the days after results, AGMs, acquisitions and leadership changes. When an answer is wrong, trace the cited source and fix the cause, not the symptom.

IR checklist for AI answers:

  • Ask five investor questions about your company on four engines today, and save the answers.
  • List every source cited and mark the outdated ones.
  • Publish a dated, plain-text summary of your latest results, linked to the filing.
  • Create or update a company facts page.
  • Add organisation schema with official identifiers.
  • Route every change through compliance sign-off.
  • Re-check the answers after every results announcement.

Questions

Can a listed company in India influence what AI engines say about it?

It can improve the odds that answers are accurate, by publishing current public disclosures in clear, readable formats and correcting errors on reference sites through proper channels. No one controls what the engines write, and all of the work must stay within SEBI rules.

Is GEO for investor relations compatible with SEBI rules?

It should be designed to be. The work uses only information already disclosed to the exchanges, adds no new claims and has company approval before anything is published. Your compliance team remains the final authority.

Why does ChatGPT show old financial results for my company?

Engines often find older, widely linked pages more easily than new filings locked in PDFs. Publishing dated, plain-text summaries of each result, linked to the filing, gives them a current source that is easy to quote.

How quickly do AI answers update after results are announced?

There is no fixed timetable. It varies by engine and by how easily the new information can be found. Check the answers in the days after each announcement and fix the sources that keep old figures alive.

Should we edit our company's Wikipedia page ourselves?

No direct edits. Request corrections on the talk page, disclose your conflict of interest and cite public sources such as exchange filings or independent coverage. Volunteer editors decide.
