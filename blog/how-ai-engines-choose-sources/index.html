How AI engines choose sources and brands

AI engines do not make up their recommendations from nothing. They read sources, weigh them and repeat what holds up. Here is what the data says about which sources count.

10 Nov 20266 min readBy the Optimizer360 team

The short answer

AI engines such as ChatGPT choose sources by retrieving pages relevant to the question and favouring those that answer it directly, come from domains they treat as reliable and agree with other sources. Citation studies show Wikipedia, Reddit, review sites and media carry heavy weight, but the shares differ by engine and shift quickly.

How does ChatGPT choose sources?

When ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google AI Overviews answers a question about products or companies, it usually follows some version of the same routine. It interprets the question, searches for relevant pages, reads a small set of them and writes an answer from what they say. Some engines show their sources as links. Others show fewer, or none.

No engine publishes its full rules. What we can see is the outcome: which domains get cited, for which kinds of question, on which engine. Several large studies have measured that outcome. They agree on the broad picture and differ on the detail, and the differences are as useful as the agreement.

What do the citation studies show?

Profound's 2025 analysis of 680 million citations found that Wikipedia accounted for 47.9% of ChatGPT's top-10 cited source share. Semrush's 2025 research found Wikipedia and Reddit were ChatGPT's two most-cited domains. Peec AI's March 2026 analysis of 30 million sources found Reddit was the number one directly cited source across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews.

Ahrefs came at the question from another angle. Its 2025 study of 75,000 brands found that brand mentions across the web correlated at 0.664 with visibility in Google AI Overviews, against 0.218 for backlinks. That is a correlation, not proof of cause. It still tells you something useful: being discussed appears to matter more than being linked to.

Taken together, the message is consistent. Engines lean on sources that are broad, community-written or independently edited, and on brands that are talked about in many places.

Why do citation shares differ by engine and change so fast?

Notice that the studies do not produce a single ranking. Profound's ChatGPT data puts Wikipedia far ahead. Peec AI's later, cross-engine data puts Reddit first. Both can be true. They measured different engines, different question sets and different time periods, with different methods.

There are practical reasons the picture keeps moving:

  • Engines update often. Retrieval systems, data partnerships and ranking rules change, sometimes without notice.
  • Each engine draws on its own pool. Google AI Overviews draws on Google Search. Other engines use different search partners or their own crawlers, so the candidate pages differ.
  • Question type matters. A definition question may favour Wikipedia. A "which one should I buy" question may favour forums and review sites. A local question may favour maps and directories.
  • Wording matters. Small changes in phrasing can change which sources are retrieved.

The lesson for a marketing team is not to chase whichever domain topped last quarter's study. Measure the sources cited for your own questions, on the engines your buyers use, and check again regularly.

How do AI engines pick which brands to recommend?

Naming a brand is a separate step from citing a source. From the answers we track across engines, a few patterns stand out.

  • Consensus. Brands named across several independent sources are named more often. One glowing page rarely outweighs ten neutral mentions elsewhere.
  • A clear entity. The engine has to be sure who you are. The same name, category, location and core facts across your site, Wikidata, directories and profiles help it resolve you correctly.
  • Fit to the question. A brand described as "for mid-size manufacturers in western India" is easier to match to that question than one described as "solutions for everyone".
  • Evidence. Reviews, numbers and independent coverage give the engine something concrete to repeat.
  • Recency. Current information tends to win over stale information, though old facts linger when nothing replaces them.

These are observed patterns, not published rules. Treat them as hypotheses to test on your own questions.

Which sources should brands in India focus on?

Start with the sources the engines already cite for your questions. For most categories the list will include some of these:

  • Wikipedia and Wikidata. An accurate article if your company meets Wikipedia's notability standards, and a clean Wikidata entity either way.
  • Reddit and Quora threads where your buyers ask questions, including city and category communities.
  • Review platforms, from Google Business Profile to category portals and B2B review sites. BrightLocal's 2025 survey found 74% of consumers use two or more review sites before they choose.
  • Trade and business media that cover your sector.
  • "Best X in Y" lists, comparison sites and newsletters.
  • Video with full transcripts, so what is said can also be read.
  • Your own site, with answer-first pages and structured data.

Our feeder network and content and community work is built around these sources. The Reddit and Wikipedia playbook covers the two biggest in detail.

A sources audit in five steps:

  1. Pick 20 to 50 buyer questions that matter to revenue.
  2. Ask each one on the engines your buyers use, and save every answer with its date.
  3. List every cited source and the brands each one names.
  4. Mark the sources that are wrong, outdated or silent about you.
  5. Choose three to fix or earn this quarter, and re-check after each change.

Can you influence which sources an engine cites?

Not directly, and no one can promise a placement. What you can do is improve the odds. Publish pages that answer the question better than anything else the engine might find. Make sure your facts are identical wherever they appear. Earn honest mentions in the places the engine already trusts.

Evidence helps. The Princeton and IIT Delhi GEO study (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) found that adding statistics, quotations and cited sources to content raised visibility in generative answers by up to about 40%.

Then measure. Our platform records every source cited in every answer it captures, so you can see which sources decide your category and whether your work is changing them.

Questions

Why does ChatGPT cite Wikipedia so often?

Wikipedia is broad, edited by volunteers under sourcing rules and structured consistently across millions of topics. Profound's 2025 analysis of 680 million citations found it made up 47.9% of ChatGPT's top-10 cited source share.

Does Perplexity use the same sources as ChatGPT?

Not exactly. Engines use different indexes and retrieval methods, so their cited sources overlap but differ. Peec AI's March 2026 study found Reddit was the top directly cited source across several engines, including Perplexity, but the mix below it varies.

Is my own website enough to get cited by AI engines?

Usually not on its own. Engines cross-check what you say against independent sources. Your site should state your facts clearly, and reviews, media, community threads and directories then confirm them.

How often do AI citation sources change?

Often enough that a one-off audit goes stale. Engines update their retrieval, and answers differ by wording and by day. A weekly or monthly re-check of your core questions is a sensible minimum.

Do backlinks help with AI citations?

They help pages get found and trusted. Ahrefs' 2025 study of 75,000 brands found brand mentions correlated at 0.664 with AI Overview visibility against 0.218 for backlinks, so mentions deserve at least as much attention as links.
