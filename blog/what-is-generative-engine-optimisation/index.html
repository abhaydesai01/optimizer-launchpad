What is generative engine optimisation (GEO)?

AI engines now answer the questions your buyers used to type into a search box. This guide explains how those answers are built, and what you can do to be in them.

10 Nov 20269 min readBy the Optimizer360 team

The short answer

Generative engine optimisation (GEO) is the practice of making a brand accurately named, described and cited in answers from AI engines such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews. It works by publishing clear, citable facts on your own pages and earning consistent mentions in the third-party sources those engines retrieve and trust.

What is generative engine optimisation?

Generative engine optimisation, or GEO, is the work of shaping what AI engines say when someone asks about your category, your competitors or you. In US usage it is spelt generative engine optimization. The aim is simple. When a buyer asks an AI engine for a shortlist, your brand is named, described correctly and backed by a source the engine can point to.

SEO asks, "Where does my page rank?" GEO asks, "What does the answer say about me, and which sources did it use?" Those are different questions. A page can rank well and still be missing from the answer. A brand can be named in the answer because of a review site, a forum thread or a news story, without its own site being cited at all.

The term is closely tied to a study by researchers at Princeton and IIT Delhi (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024), which tested how changes to content affect visibility inside generative answers. We return to its findings below.

How do AI engines put an answer together?

Each engine works differently, and none publishes its full method. The broad pattern is still consistent enough to act on.

  • Reading the question. The engine works out what is being asked: a definition, a comparison, a shortlist, a price check or a local recommendation.
  • Retrieval. For many questions, especially about current products and companies, the engine searches the web or its own index and pulls a small set of pages to read.
  • Choosing sources. It favours pages that answer the question directly, come from sources it treats as reliable, and agree with other sources.
  • Entity understanding. It tries to resolve your brand to one entity: a company with a name, a location, products, leaders and a category. Where facts conflict across sources, the answer turns vague or wrong.
  • Writing the answer. The model writes a short response, often naming a few brands in a particular order and, on some engines, linking the sources it used.

Two things follow. First, the engine is not reading only your website. It reads what others say about you, and often trusts that more. Second, it rewards clarity. A page that states a fact plainly, in one sentence, is easier to quote than a page that buries the same fact in a brochure PDF.

For a marketing head in India, the practical point is this. The shortlist is increasingly formed before anyone visits your site. 6sense research from October 2024 found that buyers are around 70% of the way through the buying process before they talk to sellers. If an AI answer shaped that first 70%, you want to know what it said, and whether it was true.

What are the levers of GEO?

Seven levers do most of the work. None of them is a trick, and all of them also help real buyers.

1. Entity facts

Decide the facts you want every engine to repeat: what you do, for whom, where, since when and who runs it. State them on one page of your site, word them the same way everywhere, and fix the places where they differ. Inconsistent facts produce vague answers.

2. Citable pages

Write pages that answer one question each, answer first. Use the buyer's own wording in headings. Include real numbers the engine can quote: sizes, dates, specifications, coverage and process steps. A clear FAQ or comparison page is often easier to cite than a home page.

3. Third-party sources

Engines cross-check. Mentions in trade media, directories, "best X in Y" lists and newsletters give them independent confirmation. Ahrefs' 2025 study of 75,000 brands found brand mentions correlated at 0.664 with AI Overview visibility, against 0.218 for backlinks. That is a correlation, not proof of cause, but it is a strong hint about where to spend effort. Our feeder network work is built for this.

4. Reviews

Reviews inform buyers and engines alike. BrightLocal's 2025 survey found 74% of consumers use two or more review sites before they choose, and only 4% never read reviews. The work is a steady flow of real reviews, answered in public, with wrong facts corrected. Never fabricated. See reputation.

5. Communities

Forums and Q&A sites are cited heavily. Peec AI's March 2026 analysis of 30 million sources found Reddit was the number one directly cited source across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews. The honest way in is to answer real questions openly as the brand. Our Reddit and Wikipedia playbook covers how.

6. Structured data

Schema markup for your organisation, products, locations and FAQs states your facts in a format machines can read without guessing. It does not guarantee a mention. It removes ambiguity, which is often what stands between a vague answer and an accurate one.

7. Freshness

Engines prefer current information, but stale facts linger when nothing replaces them. When prices, leadership, results or products change, update your pages, profiles and structured data in the same week, then check what the engines now say.

What does the research say works?

The most cited study is by researchers at Princeton and IIT Delhi (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024). They tested content changes against generative engine answers and found that adding statistics, quotations and cited sources raised a page's visibility in those answers by up to about 40%.

The finding matches common sense. An engine is trying to give a reliable answer. Content that carries evidence, names its sources and includes quotable figures is easier to trust and easier to lift into a sentence. Content that is vague, padded or purely promotional gives the engine nothing to use.

Two cautions. "Up to about 40%" is the upper end across test conditions, not a promise for every page. And engines change often, so any tactic should be measured on your own questions rather than assumed to work.

How do you measure GEO?

  1. 01

    Build a prompt universe

    List the questions your buyers actually ask, drawn from your SEO data, sales enquiries and category. AI engines publish no search volume, so rank questions by demand you can see, not invented numbers.

  2. 02

    Take a Week-1 baseline

    Run every question across the engines you care about. Record who is named, in what order and which sources are cited. Date every answer.

  3. 03

    Track four numbers

    Visibility score, citation rate, share of voice and brand sentiment. Each tells you something different about your position.

  4. 04

    Watch for drift

    Re-run after launches, price changes and results. Answers move, and wrong facts can appear quickly.

We cover this in detail in how to measure AI visibility. Our platform tracks eight AI engines with up to 10,000 prompts per brand a day, so changes show up against the baseline rather than in screenshots.

How do you start with GEO?

Start small and measurable. A sensible first month looks like this:

  1. Write down the 20 to 50 questions that matter most to revenue. Ask your sales team what buyers ask on the first call.
  2. Ask those questions in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google, and save each answer with its date.
  3. List the sources the engines cite. Note which mention you, which mention competitors and which carry wrong facts.
  4. Fix your own facts first: about page, product or service pages, FAQs, structured data and Google Business Profile.
  5. Pick two or three third-party sources to work on: a review platform, a trade publication, a community where your buyers ask questions.
  6. Re-measure after four to six weeks and compare with your first answers.

If you would rather have this run for you, our GEO service starts with a baseline and a ranked map of the questions worth owning.

GEO checklist:

  • One page that states your core facts plainly.
  • The same facts on every profile, listing and directory.
  • Answer-first pages for your top buyer questions, with real numbers.
  • Organisation, product and FAQ schema in place.
  • Real reviews, answered in public.
  • An open, named presence where your buyers ask questions.
  • A dated baseline of what AI engines say about you today.

What are the common myths about GEO?

"GEO replaces SEO." It does not. Many engines retrieve from search indexes, so pages that are crawlable and well structured still matter. GEO builds on SEO. See GEO vs SEO.

"You can buy your way into the answer." Whatever paid formats engines offer, the organic answer is shaped by the sources they read. That is what GEO works on.

"Someone can guarantee you the top spot." No one controls the engines. Answers vary by engine, by wording and by day. A promise of guaranteed rankings is a guess. What can be done is rigorous work, measured every week.

"It is keyword stuffing for AI." The research points the other way. Evidence, sources and clarity help. Padding does not.

"Only big brands get named." Specific, local and technical questions often leave room for specialists. A focused brand with clear facts can own narrow questions that a larger competitor ignores.

Questions

Is GEO the same as AEO?

They overlap heavily. Answer engine optimisation (AEO) grew out of featured snippets and voice answers, while GEO focuses on generative AI engines such as ChatGPT and Gemini. In practice the work is similar: clear facts, answer-first pages and trusted third-party sources.

How long does GEO take to show results?

It depends on the category, the competition and how many sources need fixing. In one real-estate programme, the visibility score rose from 32% to 58% across eight AI engines in under 2.5 months from the Week-1 baseline. That is one programme, not a promise.

Which AI engines should I track?

Start with the engines your buyers use. We track eight: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Grok, Perplexity, Kimi, Microsoft Copilot and Google AI Overviews, because their answers differ.

Can a small company do GEO without an agency?

Yes. The basics are a clear facts page, answer-first content, schema, real reviews and an honest community presence. An agency adds scale, measurement across engines and the sustained third-party work that is hard to run alongside a day job.

Does GEO matter for B2B companies?

Yes. G2's May 2025 survey of 1,169 B2B buyers found 29% start research on AI chat tools more often than on Google. Vendor shortlists are increasingly formed inside those answers.
