The short answer

GEO for industrial and engineering companies is the work of getting a manufacturer named, with correct specs and certifications, when buyers ask AI for suppliers. Engines draw on B2B directories, tender and spec documents, trade press, datasheets and LinkedIn. Optimizer360 measures 8 engines, turns technical facts into pages they can quote and traces each RFQ back to where it began.

What do industrial buyers ask AI?

Supplier questions from procurement, engineering and distribution.

  • "Top stainless steel valve manufacturers in India with API certification?"

    A certified-supplier shortlist where proof of certification decides inclusion.

  • "Who makes IE3 motors for export in Gujarat?"

    A capability and location question answered from directories and spec pages.

  • "Indian suppliers of ASTM A105 flanges near Pune?"

    A material-grade query that needs exact, readable specifications.

  • "EPC contractors in India with large solar plant experience?"

    A track-record question shaped by trade press and tender results.

  • "Authorised distributors of this pump brand in Kolkata?"

    A channel question that old dealer lists often get wrong.

Which sources do AI engines lean on for industrial companies?

  • B2B directories and marketplaces such as IndiaMART and TradeIndia.
  • Tender and specification documents, including public procurement portals.
  • Trade press, industry associations and exhibition lists.
  • Product datasheets and catalogues, when the text is readable.
  • Certification registers for BIS, ISO and similar standards.
  • LinkedIn company pages and engineering posts.
  • YouTube plant tours and product demos with transcripts.
  • Wikipedia for larger groups.

What usually goes wrong in AI answers for manufacturers?

  • Specs quoted from a directory listing because the real datasheet is a scanned PDF.
  • Old capacities, plant locations or lapsed certifications repeated as current.
  • The manufacturer confused with a trader who resells its products.
  • Missing from "top manufacturers" lists the engines keep quoting.
  • The distributor network out of date.

What do we do for industrial and engineering companies?

  • Brand story and GEO: product and spec pages in plain text, certifications with numbers and validity, application guides and product schema.
  • Reputation: digital PR in trade press, consistent directory listings and replies to reviews.
  • Feeder network: a technical blog, a video channel with demos and transcripts, and LinkedIn content from your engineers.
  • Content and community: open, brand-labelled answers in engineering forums and threads.
  • AI agents: a website and WhatsApp agent that answers spec questions and routes RFQs to the right engineer.
  • Leads and pipeline: each RFQ traced back to the answer, listing or article where it began.

Which rules do we work inside for industrial companies?

  • Verifiable certifications: BIS, ISO, API and similar marks are stated only while current and with evidence.
  • No borrowed approvals: no "approved by" or vendor-list claims without written proof.
  • NDAs and confidentiality: no customer names or project details without consent.
  • ASCI: performance claims backed by test data.
  • DPDP Act: RFQ and enquiry data handled with consent.

Questions

Do industrial buyers really use ChatGPT to find suppliers?

B2B buyers increasingly start research with AI chat tools, and most of the buying journey happens before they speak to a seller. We track the supplier questions in your category across 8 engines so you can see what buyers are told.

Our specs are in PDF catalogues. Is that a problem for AI engines?

Often, yes, especially with scanned PDFs. Engines then quote a directory or a reseller instead. We turn key specs and certifications into plain-text pages with schema that engines can read and cite.

Can we mention customer projects in our content?

Only with written consent. Where projects are under NDA, we show capability through specs, standards met and anonymised application examples.

Does a directory listing affect AI answers?

Directories are among the sources engines cite for supplier questions, so inconsistent listings can spread wrong facts. We make listings consistent and point them to accurate pages on your own site.
