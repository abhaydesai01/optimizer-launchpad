The short answer

GEO for B2B and professional services is the work of getting your firm named on the vendor shortlists buyers build with AI. Engines draw on company websites, LinkedIn, industry directories, trade press, award lists and Google reviews. Optimizer360 measures 8 engines, publishes expertise pages they can quote, earns the third-party mentions they trust and traces enquiries back to the answer.

What do B2B buyers ask AI?

Shortlist questions, asked long before a sales call.

  • "Best CA firm in Mumbai for startup compliance?"

    A local shortlist where reviews, directories and articles decide who is named.

  • "Top logistics partners for D2C brands in India?"

    A category list built from trade press, listicles and case pages.

  • "HR consulting firms in Bengaluru that work with manufacturers?"

    A fit question. Engines look for evidence of sector experience.

  • "Compare these two firms for GST advisory."

    A head-to-head the engine settles from whatever it can find about each.

  • "Who are the leading IT staffing companies in Pune?"

    A ranking question shaped by directories and business media.

  • "Does this firm have experience in pharma?"

    A proof question that gated PDFs cannot answer.

Which sources do AI engines lean on for B2B firms?

  • Your service, sector and insight pages, when they answer questions in plain text.
  • LinkedIn company pages, leader posts and articles.
  • Industry directories, award lists and association member lists.
  • Business media and trade press, including interviews and bylines.
  • Google reviews for local professional services.
  • Reddit and Quora threads where founders and managers ask for recommendations.
  • Podcasts and webinars with transcripts.
  • Wikipedia for larger firms.

What usually goes wrong in AI answers for B2B firms?

  • The firm described by an old service mix or an old office list.
  • Partners who have left still named as the firm's experts.
  • No sign of sector experience because case studies sit in gated PDFs.
  • Missing from "top firms" lists that the engines keep quoting.
  • Generic answers because the website has no answer-first pages.
  • Confusion with a similarly named firm in another city.

What do we do for B2B and professional services firms?

  • Brand story and GEO: our Brand and Product package for vendor shortlists, with answer-first service and sector pages and schema for the firm and its people.
  • Reputation: real client reviews where the rules allow, replies in public and digital PR in business media.
  • Feeder network: a newsletter, guide site and podcast, plus earned places on credible lists and directories.
  • Content and community: thought-leadership content and open answers in founder and finance communities.
  • AI agents: a website agent that answers scope questions, qualifies enquiries and books calls.
  • Leads and pipeline: every mandate traced back to the answer, article or thread where it began.

Which rules do we work inside for professional services?

  • Professional body codes: Bar Council of India rules restrict advocates from advertising or soliciting work, and ICAI's code limits solicitation by chartered accountants. For these firms we keep content informational and within the code.
  • Client confidentiality: no client names or matter details without written consent.
  • ASCI: no claims we cannot evidence.
  • DPDP Act: enquiry data handled with consent.

Questions

How do I get my firm on ChatGPT's shortlist for my service?

Publish clear pages on what you do, for whom and with what evidence, then earn mentions in the directories, lists and media the engines cite for your category. We find those sources first, so the work goes where the answers come from.

Can a law firm or CA firm do GEO under professional rules?

Yes, within limits. We keep content informational and factual, avoid solicitation and follow the relevant professional body code, with your sign-off on everything.

Does LinkedIn content show up in AI answers?

Public LinkedIn pages and articles are sometimes cited, depending on the category and the engine. Our Sources view shows whether they matter for your questions before we invest effort there.

Do we have to name our clients in content?

No. Expertise can be shown through sector pages, anonymised examples and published thinking. We never name a client without written consent.

How do we know if an enquiry came from an AI answer?

We trace each lead from first mention to closed deal across AI answers, reviews, threads and content, and report it alongside SEO and owned enquiries.
